Offboarding Policy

Document ID: POL-OFB-011
Effective Date: January 1, 2025
Last Reviewed: September 1, 2025
Owner: People Operations
Version: 1.8

1. Purpose and Scope

This policy defines the structured process for separating employees from Acme Corp, whether voluntarily (resignation) or involuntarily (termination, layoff, end of contract). A well-executed offboarding protects business continuity, safeguards company assets and data, fulfills legal obligations, and ensures a respectful departure experience.

This policy applies to all regular full-time and part-time employees. For contractor and vendor offboarding, see the Vendor & Third-Party Management Policy (POL-VND-019 §7).

Related policies (ownership boundaries): - Equipment return procedures: POL-EQP-007 §8 - Accrued PTO payout rules: POL-PTO-002 §5 - COBRA continuation coverage: POL-BEN-005 §3.4 - System access revocation timelines: POL-SEC-004 §3.2

2. Types of Separation

2.1 Voluntary Resignation

An employee-initiated separation. The employee submits written notice to their manager and People Operations.

2.2 Involuntary Termination — With Cause

A company-initiated separation resulting from policy violations, misconduct, or failure on a Performance Improvement Plan. The disciplinary process in POL-WPC-009 §11 governs the decision-making; this policy governs the administrative offboarding steps that follow.

2.3 Involuntary Termination — Without Cause / Layoff

A company-initiated separation not attributable to employee fault, typically due to restructuring, role elimination, or business downturn. A written separation agreement including severance terms (see Section 8) is standard.

2.4 Mutual Separation

A negotiated separation agreed upon by both parties. Terms are governed by a written mutual separation agreement reviewed by Legal.

3. Notice Periods

3.1 Employee-Provided Notice

Employees are asked to provide the following advance written notice of resignation:

Level Expected Notice Period
Individual Contributor 2 weeks (10 business days)
Manager / Senior IC 3 weeks (15 business days)
Director and above 4 weeks (20 business days)

These are professional expectations, not legally enforceable minimums (except where a written employment contract specifies otherwise). Employees who provide less notice will be noted in their personnel file; it will not affect accrued benefit payouts required by law.

3.2 Garden Leave

Acme Corp reserves the right to place a resigning employee on garden leave for all or part of their notice period — meaning they are paid but not required (or permitted) to perform work or access company systems. Garden leave decisions are made by the employee's VP and HR.

3.3 Company-Initiated Separations

For company-initiated separations, Acme Corp may provide: - Working notice: The employee continues in their role for a defined period. - Pay in lieu of notice: The employee is released immediately but paid through the notice period. - Immediate release: Standard for terminations for cause; no additional notice pay unless required by state law.

4. Notification Responsibilities

4.1 Triggering the Offboarding Workflow

Upon receipt of a resignation or decision to terminate, the manager must notify People Operations within 1 business day via the Workday Separation Initiation form. People Operations then triggers all downstream offboarding tasks.

4.2 Internal Notifications

People Operations coordinates the following notifications: - IT Security: Schedules access revocation for the last day (see POL-SEC-004 §3.2 — access is revoked within 4 hours of separation) - Payroll: Ensures final paycheck is processed correctly - Benefits: Initiates COBRA notification and HSA/FSA close-out - IT Assets: Triggers equipment return process (see POL-EQP-007 §8) - Finance: Flags any open expense reports or outstanding advances

4.3 Team Notification

The departing employee's manager is responsible for communicating the departure to the team. Timing and messaging should be agreed upon with People Operations. People Operations provides a standard communication template. Sensitive separations (terminations for cause) should follow HR guidance closely on what to disclose.

5. Knowledge Transfer

5.1 Obligation

All departing employees are required to complete a Knowledge Transfer Checklist in Workday before their last day. The checklist scope is proportional to seniority and role complexity.

5.2 Knowledge Transfer Checklist Requirements

At minimum, every departing employee must document or hand off: - Status of all active projects, tasks, and open items - Location of key files, documentation, and institutional knowledge (Confluence pages, shared drives) - Credentials or access they uniquely hold (submitted to IT, not to a colleague) - Ongoing vendor or client relationships (introduced to successor or manager) - Recurring processes, reports, or deadlines they own

The manager is responsible for reviewing and signing off the Knowledge Transfer Checklist no later than 2 business days before the last day.

5.3 Successor Briefing

Where a successor has been identified, the departing employee is expected to participate in structured handover meetings during the notice period. The format and depth are agreed between the manager and employee.

6. Exit Interview

6.1 Purpose

Exit interviews are conducted by People Operations (not the departing employee's manager) to gather candid feedback on the employee experience, reasons for leaving, and suggestions for improvement. Participation is voluntary but strongly encouraged.

6.2 Format

Exit interviews are offered in two formats: - Live interview: 30-minute video or phone call with an HR Business Partner - Written survey: Anonymous online questionnaire via Workday

Employees may choose either format or both.

6.3 Confidentiality

Exit interview responses are treated confidentially. Specific individual feedback is not shared with the departing employee's manager. Aggregated, anonymized themes are shared with leadership quarterly to inform retention and culture initiatives.

7. Last Day Checklist

7.1 Employee Actions on Last Day

On the final day of employment, the departing employee must: - [ ] Return all company equipment (or confirm shipment initiated for remote employees) per POL-EQP-007 §8.1 - [ ] Transfer any personal files from company devices to personal storage before system access ends - [ ] Complete and submit any outstanding expense reports in Expensify (per POL-EXP-001 §2.2) - [ ] Complete the Knowledge Transfer Checklist in Workday - [ ] Return any physical access cards, parking passes, or keys to People Operations or office manager

7.2 Company Actions on Last Day

People Operations and IT will: - Revoke all system access and deactivate accounts within 4 hours of the separation effective time - Redirect the employee's email to their manager for a transition period (maximum 90 days, subject to privacy considerations) - Initiate equipment return for remote employees via prepaid shipping - Process the final paycheck

8. Final Paycheck and Severance

8.1 Final Paycheck Timing

Final paycheck timing is governed by the applicable state's wage payment laws:

Separation Type Common State Requirements
Voluntary resignation Within 72 hours to next regular payday (varies by state)
Involuntary termination Immediately or next business day in most states

People Operations maintains a state-by-state final pay schedule. When in doubt, Payroll defaults to immediate issuance to minimize legal exposure.

8.2 Final Paycheck Contents

The final paycheck includes: - Base salary through the last day worked - Any earned but unpaid commissions or bonuses (per plan terms) - Accrued, unused PTO payout per POL-PTO-002 §5 and applicable state law

8.3 Severance

Severance is not guaranteed and is not provided for terminations for cause or voluntary resignations. For layoffs or company-initiated separations without cause, severance eligibility is as follows:

Years of Service Severance
Less than 1 year 4 weeks base salary
1–3 years 6 weeks base salary
3–7 years 8 weeks base salary
7+ years 12 weeks base salary

Severance is conditioned on the employee signing a written Separation Agreement and General Release reviewed by Legal. Employees are advised to consult personal legal counsel before signing.

8.4 Severance and Benefits Continuation

During the severance period, Acme Corp will continue to pay the employer portion of health insurance premiums if the employee remains on the Acme Corp health plan. After the severance period ends, COBRA continuation coverage is available at the employee's expense (see POL-BEN-005 §3.4).

9. Post-Separation Obligations

9.1 Confidentiality

The obligation to protect Acme Corp's Confidential and Restricted information (as defined in POL-SEC-004 §2.1) survives separation indefinitely. This includes trade secrets, customer data, product roadmaps, and employee information.

9.2 Non-Solicitation

For a period of 12 months following separation, former employees may not solicit Acme Corp employees or customers with whom they had direct business contact during their employment, subject to applicable state law.

9.3 Non-Compete

Non-compete obligations, if any, are governed by the terms of the individual's employment offer letter or any separately executed agreement, and are subject to applicable state law. Many states (including California) do not enforce non-competes; People Operations or Legal can clarify applicability.

9.4 Return of Confidential Information

Former employees must return or certify destruction of all confidential documents, files, and materials (physical and digital) in their possession upon separation, including any materials stored on personal devices or cloud accounts.

10. Alumni Program

Employees who depart in good standing (no termination for cause) are eligible to join the Acme Corp Alumni Network, which provides: - Access to the alumni Slack community and job board - Eligibility for rehire (subject to standard hiring process; prior tenure may be recognized) - Invitations to select company events

Alumni network access is revoked for former employees who violate post-separation obligations.
